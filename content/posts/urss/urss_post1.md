The following will form a "rough and ready" pedagogical introduction to the sort of membrane mechanics that I will be doing in the course of this project. It is by no means an extensive review (see for instance the excellent paper by Deserno) but will contain the bare minima of results and techniques, primarily from differential geometry, that I will reference in future posts. 

## What is a membrane?

We can motivate the following definition, a membrane is a 2d manifold, smoothly embedded in $\mathbb{R}^3$, we represent this with a parametrization:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bm{X}:\,U \subset \mathbb{R}^2 &\longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^3 \\
       (u,v) &\mapsto \bm{X}(u,v) 
\end{aligned}
$$
